Halo privacy policy

Effective date: 10 November 2026

Halo is a floating app switcher for Android, developed by Yogesh Paliyal. In short: Halo does not collect, store on a server, share or sell any of your data. The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything off your phone.

What Halo keeps on your phone

Halo saves only its own settings, in the app's private storage on your device:

  • the apps you picked as favourites, and their order
  • where the bubble sits on the screen, and whether it's turned on
  • your gesture settings: tap action, hold delay and haptics

This information never leaves your phone through Halo. It is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage.

Android backup: if you have Android's device backup turned on, Android may include these settings in your phone's backup to your Google account, so they come back if you reinstall. That backup is run by Android and Google under Google's privacy policy, not by Halo. You can turn it off in your phone's backup settings.

Permissions and why Halo needs them

PermissionWhat Halo uses it for
Display over other appsDraws the bubble and the app menu on top of other apps.
Usage access (optional)Finds which apps you opened recently, to fill the outer ring of the menu, and, only if you pick apps to hide the bubble in, which app is on screen. Halo reads this on your phone as it needs it and does not save or send it. Without this permission, Halo shows favourites only.
Foreground serviceKeeps the bubble running while it's turned on.
NotificationsShows a silent notification while the bubble is on, with a Stop button.
Run at startupTurns the bubble back on after your phone restarts or Halo updates, but only if it was on before.
List installed appsShows your apps so you can choose favourites, and opens the app you pick. The list stays on your phone.

Halo has no internet, location, contacts, camera, microphone, storage or accessibility permissions.

Analytics, ads and tracking

Halo has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code, and no account or sign-in.

Where you download Halo

The store or site you install Halo from may collect information under its own policy. For example, Google Play and GitHub keep download statistics. As the developer, I only see totals, such as the number of downloads or installs, never who you are.

Children

Halo isn't directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. The full history is in the repository's commit log.

Contact

Questions or concerns: open an issue on GitHub.
